The premise

Nobody ever became a better manager from a slide deck.

High-end training runs into thousands of pounds a day, and most of it evaporates the moment people get back to their desks. You can measure attendance, completion and satisfaction. You can't measure whether anyone actually got better — and the content library left behind goes unread.

Capability develops through real workplace challenges, not the one-off event. The workshop is the catalyst; the real learning happens back at the gemba — the place where work happens and value is created. And the timing is right: when people need help before a hard meeting now, they ask an AI, not a content library.

Three principles hold it together

Learning where work happens

The workplace is the classroom. We capture real development events — a hard conversation, a presentation, managing a poor performer — not simulated exercises.

Guidance, not management

This is not appraisal, rating or bonus. There's no pass or fail. Everything exists to build capability — which is exactly why people will actually use it.

Minimal friction

People hate surveys, forms and reflective journals. The learner brings the experience; the platform builds the structure. Capture is voice or a short AI conversation, never boxes to fill.
